# Applied Messaging Guide v2

Status: PROPOSED / NOT APPROVED

Research cutoff: 2026-08-19

This guide applies [Messaging Core](MESSAGING_CORE.md). It provides working patterns for
drafts. It does not approve final copy, visual design, product claims, translations or
publication.

## Copy hierarchy

Use this order when the surface supports it:

1. Recognition: the external change or directly evidenced concern.
2. Relief: the burden that can credibly move away from the customer.
3. Mechanism or proof: why the relief is believable.
4. Control and reassurance: what the customer sees, decides, corrects, or approves.
5. Action: the real next step.

Do not use copy hierarchy to prescribe visual style. Its only visual implication is priority: the leading meaning, proof, reassurance, and action should not compete equally.

## Working length constraints

These are editorial starting points, not behavioral laws or universal limits. Natural language, legal clarity, accessibility, product truth, and locale expansion take precedence.

| Element | Working constraint | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eyebrow | Optional, 2 to 6 words | Orient only when audience or context would otherwise be unclear. |
| Headline | One idea, often 3 to 9 words | Make recognition or relief recoverable at a glance. |
| Support line | One or two short sentences, often 12 to 32 words total | Add bounded work, audience fit, or control. |
| Proof line | One specific fact or evidence state | Substantiate the nearest claim without a proof dump. |
| Primary CTA | Specific action, often 2 to 5 words | Tell the reader what will happen next. |
| Reassurance | One material boundary or control point | Reduce uncertainty without generic comfort language. |
| Section opening | Headline plus 1 to 3 short sentences | Add only the context needed for the section's job. |

Do not force translated copy into English counts. Do not delete a scope or responsibility boundary to hit a number.

## Roles of the core elements

### Headline

Carry one felt concern or one credible relief. Do not carry a category definition, mechanism list, named provider, competitive claim, and outcome promise at once.

### Support line

Explain who the offer is for only when needed, what supported work Obenan carries, and the practical relief. If approval or a material limit changes the meaning, state it here or in immediate reassurance.

### Proof

Use current evidence close to the claim: a dated fact, supported scope, claim state, verified customer statement, case with custody, process artifact, receipt, or authoritative read-back. A first-party product page proves a public claim, not independent delivery.

### CTA

Describe the destination or action truthfully. Use one dominant action. Add a quieter alternative only when it serves a distinct readiness need. Do not hide commitment, cost, data use, or a form behind low-pressure wording.

### Reassurance

Reassurance should answer a real trust question: what stays in the customer's control, what is supported, what happens next, what is visible, or what is not promised. Generic "peace of mind" cannot replace evidence.

## Surface patterns

### Homepage hero

- Lead with AI change or a validated relief, not category machinery.
- Use the support line to connect relief to bounded ongoing work.
- Give one action visual priority.
- Put one credible proof or control signal close enough to prevent an autonomy overread.
- Do not force explicit failure stakes into the hero.

Working pattern: recognition -> relief -> bounded support -> action -> proof.

### Solution pages

- Start with the customer job or concern specific to the page.
- Explain the supported object, cadence, channel, and boundary before using a high-risk verb.
- Separate what Obenan observes, recommends, prepares, executes, delivers, and verifies.
- Show exceptions, approvals, local variation, and failure handling where they affect fit.
- Put named compatibility details in proof sections, not the emotional lead.

### Free-value or assessment navigation

A free-value path should help someone understand their current state without shaming them into a sale.

Use a name only if it:

- states the actual object or output;
- matches the work performed and the evidence returned;
- does not imply a score, diagnosis, audit, fix, or recommendation the experience cannot provide;
- distinguishes informational value from a sales conversation;
- makes data use, timing, commitment, and next step clear; and
- transcreates safely across intended locales.

Do not lock a name until the route, output, proof, privacy treatment, and customer language are verified.

### Demo and conversion pages

- State exactly what the visitor will see or receive.
- Reduce irrelevant navigation and choice only if meaningful alternatives and exits remain available.
- Use the visitor's data only with clear purpose and consent.
- Do not present a simulated, prepared, or sample action as delivered or live.
- Close with the real commitment, owner, timing, and follow-up.

### Sales presentations

- Open with the buyer's evidenced concern or desired outcome, not the company history.
- Show Obenan as guide through relevant proof, not superlatives.
- Use mechanics after the audience understands why they matter.
- Keep business outcome, supported capability, customer responsibility, and external dependency distinct.
- Use observed customer facts only with provenance. Ask rather than infer objections or motives.
- End with a specific next decision and the evidence needed to make it.

### Communication design

For email, one-to-one messages, campaigns, reports, and service updates:

- lead with the recipient-relevant fact or change;
- name the action, object, owner, and time;
- keep one main job per message;
- preserve literal commercial and legal terms;
- avoid invented urgency, identity pressure, and fake scarcity; and
- never infer intent from silence, opens, clicks, or response time.

Service recovery follows: own, name, act, time, choice, evidence. Warmth never substitutes for remedy or proof.

### Product UI

- Labels should reflect the actual state and next action.
- Keep observed, inferred, recommended, prepared, approved, delivered, and verified distinct.
- Show partial, failed, expired, revoked, rolled-back, and unknown states.
- Confirmation language must name the target, scope, consequence, and reversibility.
- Error copy should say what happened, what is affected, what remains safe, what can be done, and who owns the next step.
- Do not use personhood, omniscience, or certainty to make automation feel friendly.

### Longer editorial content

- Start with a real question, observation, or customer concern.
- Distinguish evidence, interpretation, proposal, and limitation.
- Explain necessary mechanisms without turning the reader into a student who failed to keep up.
- Cite primary or peer-reviewed sources close to material claims.
- End with a useful implication or optional next step, not a fear-based conversion turn.

## Transformations

All examples in this section are illustrative and noncanonical. Product-dependent rewrites still require claim proof.

| Mechanical or instructional pattern | Peer-to-peer direction | What changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category-first instruction about mastering an internal term | "See what Obenan can handle for you." | Moves from category teaching to practical help. |
| Interface-first claim about monitoring every channel | "See what changed and what still needs you." | Leads with visibility and control, not machinery. |
| Universal autonomy claim about an entire digital presence | "Let Obenan carry the supported routine work." | Removes universal scope and total autonomy. |
| Optimization claim tied to an uncontrolled discovery outcome | "Keep supported business information current as AI changes." | Replaces an outcome frame with a bounded input. |
| Invitation to learn how a platform works | "See the work, the limits, and the next step." | Makes evaluation useful and adult-to-adult. |

## Named-platform de-anchoring

Do not swap one provider name for a list of providers. Change the level of the message.

| Platform-anchored draft behavior | De-anchoring move | Illustrative noncanonical result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leads with a provider and a ranking outcome | Name the durable customer concern | "Keep supported business information current as AI changes." |
| Defines Obenan by an integration | Name the work and retained control | "Obenan prepares the supported work and shows you what needs approval." |
| Lists channels to prove breadth | Put current compatibility in a proof layer | "See where Obenan is supported and what it can do there." |
| Treats one interface as the whole journey | State the value first, then the access route | "Keep the work moving through a supported way of working." |

Named compatibility can appear later when it is current, relevant, and exact. It is evidence, not the emotional promise.

## Assumption removal

All rewrites below are illustrative and noncanonical.

| Invented assumption pattern | Assumption-free rewrite |
| --- | --- |
| Diagnoses the customer as overwhelmed | "AI keeps changing." |
| Declares that the customer's team cannot keep up | "You do not have to follow every change alone." |
| Judges the customer's current work as wasteful | "Supported recurring work does not have to sit with you." |
| Assumes the customer needs a specialist team | "See what Obenan can carry and what stays with you." |
| Blames the customer's current tools | "See whether Obenan fits the way you work." |

## Agentic claim gates

Before a draft uses a material capability verb, complete the claim record.

| Gate | Required answer |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Who performs or supervises the work? |
| Object | What exact item is affected? |
| Cadence | Is the work point-in-time, scheduled, triggered, or continuously monitored? |
| Channel | Where can Obenan inspect, prepare, act, deliver, or only advise? |
| Authority | What permission exists, for whom, for how long, and how is it revoked? |
| Customer control | What can the customer review, correct, approve, pause, stop, reverse, or escalate? |
| Evidence | What artifact, receipt, or read-back proves the state? |
| Reliability | What coverage, failure, retry, timeout, and exception evidence exists? |
| Boundary | What remains outside Obenan or depends on another system? |
| Recovery | Who owns failure, and what are the tested rollback or escalation paths? |

Verb-specific minimums:

- Monitors requires repeated observations, cadence, coverage, current health, blind spots, and escalation. One check is not monitoring.
- Prepares requires a versioned draft, provenance, validation, and an explicit no-execution state.
- Acts requires scoped identity, permission, authorization context, confirmation where needed, receipt, audit, and recovery.
- Fixes requires a proven defect, applied change, independent postcondition, regression evidence, and residual limits.
- Handles requires defined end-to-end scope, expected-case coverage, exception ownership, receipts, and verification.
- Delivers requires the exact target, version, time, and provider or system receipt.

If a gate is missing, lower the verb to the proven state or hold the claim.

## Locale and transcreation requirements

Write one semantic brief and seven native solutions for English, Spanish, German, Dutch, Turkish, Portuguese Brazil, and Portuguese Portugal.

- Preserve AI change, bounded ongoing help, customer control, no guarantees, and platform neutrality.
- Do not translate the English chase metaphor literally as the governing concept.
- Decide form of address and politeness by target market and audience.
- Define the Spanish target market before drafting.
- Keep Portuguese Brazil and Portuguese Portugal as separate writing, review, QA, and approval streams.
- Use two qualified native reviewers: one transcreator and one independent semantic reviewer.
- Test what readers believe Obenan does, does not do, controls, and requires approval for.
- Allow natural expansion, compounds, and line reflow. Test real strings on narrow mobile and desktop.
- Reopen review when semantic intent, product scope, responsibility, or supported access changes.

## Preflight checklist

- [ ] Customer is the hero and Obenan is the guide.
- [ ] The lead names recognition or relief, not machinery.
- [ ] No prospect biography, emotion, skill, staffing, or tool use is invented.
- [ ] One dominant meaning and one truthful next action are clear.
- [ ] Every material verb has a complete, current claim record.
- [ ] Product state, approval, delivery, and verification are not collapsed.
- [ ] Proof sits close to the claim it supports.
- [ ] Customer control, limits, exceptions, and failure states remain visible.
- [ ] No named provider anchors the leading customer message.
- [ ] No guarantee, shame, fear, status pressure, or false scarcity appears.
- [ ] Commercial, legal, privacy, and security terms remain literal and accurate.
- [ ] All examples remain marked noncanonical until exact approval.
- [ ] Each locale has native semantic review and rendered QA.
- [ ] Seven has approved the exact promotion candidate.

## Rejection checklist

Reject or hold a draft if it:

- tells the prospect they are behind, overwhelmed, unskilled, or failing;
- teaches a category before offering useful recognition and relief;
- leads with a dashboard, platform, tool, listing, review, directory, or named provider;
- says or implies everything, every channel, always, no oversight, or unrestricted autonomy;
- guarantees discovery, recommendation, ranking, indexing, revenue, bookings, or external execution;
- uses fear, competitor panic, urgency theatre, status pressure, or invented scarcity;
- relabels price, contract, permission, data use, or risk to make it feel softer;
- infers customer intent from silence, clicks, opens, latency, posture, or expression;
- presents a prepared artifact as approved, delivered, verified, or live;
- removes a responsibility boundary to become shorter or more emotional; or
- treats translation, route existence, preview, or publication as locale approval.
